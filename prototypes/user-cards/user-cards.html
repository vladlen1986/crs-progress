CRS · User Management · card grid

Ten quieter cards

Same tokens, same data, same grid — ten ways to carry it with less noise. Nothing here introduces a colour, radius or font that isn't already in the CRS design system.

What the current card is doing

  1. Three org facts compete at equal weight. Department, job title and CRS role are genuinely different things — Hakan Dagtas is a Casino Manager in CRS and Chief Operating Officer by title — but set at three sizes in three greys they read as one blurred block. This screen governs access, so the CRS role is the operative fact; department and title are context.
  2. "Active" on 23 of 24 cards isn't information. Only the exception carries signal. Marking every row spends ink to say nothing.
  3. Twenty-four buttons, twenty-four equal calls to action. If the card's only action is "open it", the card is the button.
  4. The photo is the loudest element and the weakest sorting key. Nobody scans a roster by face — they scan by name, then confirm by face.
  5. Two badges float on a second plane. Overlays on the image mean every card is read twice: once as picture, once as text.
  6. The ID shares a baseline with the name and swings from 9 to 15 characters, so no two cards align down the column.

How to pick

They're not ten flavours of the same idea — they answer different questions. V1, V5 and V7 are for getting through a long list of accounts. V2 and V3 are for keeping the photographs. V6 is for working with the records themselves. V4, V8 and V10 are single moves you can lift into any of the others: status as an edge, department as a header, colour reserved for exceptions.

Three of them combine well — V1's layout with V4's rail and V10's exception rule is probably the most corporate result available from this set.

Photo slots are placeholders. All colour, radius and type tokens come from design/CRS-design-system.md — no new values introduced. Cards are the click target throughout; each has a visible focus ring.
